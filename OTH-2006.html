Retro Review: Season 4, Episode 4 – "Can't Stop This Thing We've Started"
Posted by: TVFanatic_04 • October 18, 2006 • Comments (47) • Permalink • Print

Oh my god, you guys. One Tree Hill just took a massive detour into straight-up horror movie territory, and I am still shaking.

After weeks of Peyton talking to her alleged half-brother "Derek" online, we finally got the big face-to-face introduction tonight. And let’s just say, the vibe check is completely off. But to really understand why tonight’s ending is so deeply disturbing, we have to look back at the history of Peyton’s bedroom webcam—and the terrifyingly naive way this show has treated it up until now.

The Myth of "Punk & Disorderly"

For three seasons, Peyton’s 24/7 webcam has been framed as the ultimate symbol of indie-cool authenticity. It was her digital diary. We watched her paint angsty murals on her walls, broadcast underground rock music, and vent to her audio journals. To us—and to her—the webcam was a beautiful, edgy sanctuary for lonely misfits.

Even when the webcam caused drama, it was treated like a joke. Remember in Season 1 when Brooke accidentally saw Peyton and Lucas fooling around on the live feed? The outrage was entirely about the boyfriend-stealing, not the fact that anyone on earth with an internet connection could have witnessed that exact same intimate moment. For years, Peyton completely dissolved the walls of her bedroom, trading her physical privacy for digital validation. Her dad didn't care, her friends didn't care, and we didn't care.

Tonight, that naivety caught up with us.

The Good Brother... Or the Ultimate Lurker?

When "Derek" (played by Matt Barr) first shows up, he seems like a dream. He’s a charming Marine who steps right into the protective big-brother role Peyton has desperately needed since Ellie, her birth mom, died. When some creeps corner Peyton at a club, Derek steps in and beats them to a pulp.

At first, I was cheering. But did anyone else notice how... intense that fight was? It wasn't just a protective brother thing; it felt completely unhinged.

The Ending Twist That Exposed the Danger of Streaming

If you weren't sure whether to trust him, the final two minutes of the episode absolutely confirmed our worst fears.

The camera cuts to Derek’s apartment, and my jaw literally hit the floor. The walls are completely covered in printouts, photos, and screenshots of Peyton taken directly from her Punk & Disorderly stream. He’s got her schedule, her art, everything. He isn't her brother at all. He’s "WATCHMEWATCHU"—the anonymous user from her chat room.

The absolute chills I got watching him stare at her live feed while standing in a physical shrine dedicated to her room... It turns out Peyton’s curated vulnerability wasn't just art; it was a literal blueprint for a stalker.

Why This Episode Changes Everything

This is a massive wake-up call for early internet culture. We've spent years romanticizing Peyton's webcam as this deep, creative outlet, entirely blind to the dark side of live-streaming. Peyton thought she was building a community, but she was actually feeding a parasite. She thinks she just let family into her life, but she just opened the door to a monster.

★★★★☆   4.5/5 Stars

Creepy, fast-paced, and a terrifying reality check on digital safety. Lock your doors, Peyton!
