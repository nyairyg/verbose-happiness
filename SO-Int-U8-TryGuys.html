The Try Guys built their audience around a premise: take on experiences that are , awkward, difficult, or simply outside their comfort zone and see what happens. The first emerged at BuzzFeed in 2014, with Keith Habersberger, Zach Kornfeld, Eugene Lee Yang, and Ned Fulmer forming the lineup. Their ranged from cooking and food challenges to attempts at unfamiliar jobs, physical tests, and socially uncomfortable situations – their formats rely on reality, comedy, and challenges with no demands of more expensive genres.

Years later, the group expanded beyond YouTube. In May 2024, they 2nd Try, a subscription streaming service priced at $4.99 per month or $49.99 annually. Subscribers were to receive material that does not appear in the same form on YouTube, including exclusive and uncensored videos, unedited , live streams, and early to new episodes.

The launch was preceded by of preparation: the team looked at independent media companies such as Dropout, consulted people with experience subscription-based networks, and grew the creative and cast before introducing the service.

As per their model, YouTube remained an important part of the offering rather than being replaced. The existing library stayed , and the group releasing substantial, polished episodes on the platform. Paying for 2nd Try was instead positioned around additional access and greater creative . Throughout the process, they emphasized their relationship with the audience: they explained openly what the subscription makes possible and expressed appreciation for the community that had supported them.
