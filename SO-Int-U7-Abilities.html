Schoolteachers may be experts their subjects, but they aren't always right about their students. Some of the most brilliant people in British history were at school and got terrible reports.

In his school report, John Lennon was described as ' — certainly on the road to failure'. Winston Churchill, future Prime Minister and probably Britain's most politician, clearly had a lot as a student, but his behaviour was so bad that his teacher wrote, 'I really don't know what to do.'

Stephen Fry's teacher didn't realise Fry had an English. A fine actor, comedian and writer, Fry was bottom of his class. Robert Graves, a schoolboy writer who became one of Britain's greatest poets, left school with this message from his headmaster, 'Well, goodbye, Graves — and remember that your best friend is the wastepaper basket.'

One of Diana, Princess of Wales's teachers was correct in that Diana wasn't a student, but the teacher also wrote, interestingly, that she 'must try to be less emotional … with others.'
